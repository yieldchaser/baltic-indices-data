# Coronavirus to cut multipurpose cargo demand growth prospects

*04 Mar 2020*

Initial analysis by Drewry suggests that the coronavirus (Covid-19) outbreak will lead to weaker than expected full-year cargo demand growth for multipurpose and heavlift (MPV/HL) vessels, as any anticipated recovery will not be sufficient to offset contractions of the first part of the year.

However, due to the uncertainty around containment and what that means for the global economy, there are a number of possible scenarios for this sector. These range from a relatively stable best case where the global economy picks up quickly and there is very little after-effect, to a nightmare worst-case of a global pandemic that causes recession in a number of the world’s economies (see table below).

Drewry’s baseline impact scenario assumes that the coronavirus is contained in China before 2Q20, but spreads more rapidly in other countries. This is expected to cause a significant slowdown in global GDP growth. The anticipated recovery for the global economy is pushed back to 2021 and is likely to be smaller in scale as some of the pent-up demand will have vanished.

The IMF recently downgraded its 2020 GDP growth projection for China to 5.4%, even with a strong rebound in 2Q20 as the Chinese government is expected to unleash a large fiscal stimulus program to kick-start the economy. And as recently as Monday 2nd March the OECD lowered its central growth forecast for the global economy from 2.9% to 2.4%, indicating that a longer lasting and more intensive coronavirus outbreak could slash growth to just 1.5% in 2020.

For the MPV/HL sector, there is not only the effect of the virus on project cargo (both from and to China) to consider, there is also the impact on the competing sectors of dry bulk and container shipping.

Drewry’s baseline view is that for the container sector the impact has just about been bearable but the longer and more widespread the Covid-19 outbreak, the more damage it will cause. The more widespread and protracted the situation, the less likely it is that the container shipping market will be able register growth this year, while a weakened global economy would hold less pent up demand, making for a softer recovery in 2021.

For the dry bulk sector, where China accounts for 34% of all dry bulk trade and has some 56% of newbuildings on its orderbook, the initial impact has been more severe. Freight rates dropped significantly over the first two months of the year with the Baltic Daily Index touching a four-year low in February. Although the spot market has started to pick up slightly, there is little optimism for the future strength. Activity in the service sector has started to rebound but travel restrictions have kept construction and manufacturing activity muted, with a knock-on effect on the commodity markets. All of which suggests a subdued dry bulk market for 2020 and recovery more likely in 2021.

For the MPV sector, steel and renewables are some of the largest cargoes for this region. Chinese domestic demand for steel is not expected to pick up before April, but this has led to historically high inventory levels at many mills and an increased interest in exports.

For the renewables sector, construction of both wind and solar projects in China has slowed over the last two months due to the coronavirus outbreak hampering the return to work after the Lunar New Year holidays. However, recent reports suggest that turbine production is on the increase again in China.

Our assumptions for breakbulk and project cargo are that although we expect some Chinese (overseas) projects to be delayed, there is clear momentum for most and there is some return to export demand. However, this will be hampered by the latest news of outbreaks in the Middle East, a significant region for project cargo.

It is our opinion that for the short term the knock on effect from container and dry bulk segments will be detrimental to the breakbulk sector, as the supply of vessels able to carry general cargo will increase. At the same time multipurpose cargo demand will be weaker due to the loss of Chinese exports and investor confidence is likely to soften as well, due to concerns for the global economy.

In the medium term the increasing spread of the outbreak, the loss of investor confidence coupled with the tightening of financing costs for Chinese companies and weaker global demand are all negative drivers for this sector. Weaker than expected demand over 2Q20 will delay the recovery we were expecting this year.

It is also worth mentioning here that although both the container and bulk sectors have a very small upside due to vessels being out of service due to scrubber refits, there is no such respite for the MPV/HL sector as so few vessels were taking this option.

Given this turn of events Drewry will be downgrading its full-year cargo demand forecast for multipurpose vessels from its previous projection of 1.8% growth, when it publishes its next forecast at the end of March.

Our previous forecast had some concerns around cargo demand in 2021, due to increased supply in the container and bulk sectors, thereby increasing the competition for breakbulk demand. It is possible that the pent-up demand in 2021, even if it is subdued, could remove some of that extra competition. We believe this could make our previously predicted drop in MPV/HL demand over 2021 less severe.

If containment is delayed into 2H20 and Covid-19 continues to spread, the hit to multipurpose shipping and the wider global economy will be much greater over both this year and next.

Drewry will be updating its 5-year forecasts, including full analysis of the coronavirus impacts in the next edition of the Multipurpose Forecaster report which is due to be published at the end of March 2020.
