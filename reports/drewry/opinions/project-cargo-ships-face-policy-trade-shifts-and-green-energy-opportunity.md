# Project cargo ships face policy, trade shifts and green energy opportunity

*16 Sep 2025*

Investment growth in global renewable energy remains variable, declining sharply in the US due to policy shifts and regulatory uncertainty while it is accelerating in Europe due to its clean energy transition through major decarbonisation initiatives. Project Carriers are well-positioned to benefit from rising demand in Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia, especially as net-zero emissions gain momentum over the next five-to-ten years.

Global investment in renewable energy increased to $386 billion in 1H25, a 10% jump YoY, mainly driven by a surge in offshore wind and small-scale solar power. Meanwhile, funding for large-scale utility solar and onshore wind projects dropped 13% compared to 1H24. China remained the leader, reflecting a shift in investor preferences towards more predictable returns amid policy and market uncertainty.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research

Europe witnessed a substantial rise in clean energy investment; for instance, investment by EU-27 spiked 63% (Source: Bloomberg) in 1H25 compared to 2H24, but investment in renewables in the US slumped 36% during the same period. Notably, there has been a surge in wind energy investment, driven by robust policy frameworks and long-term incentives. Overall, global investment continues to grow, but it varies by region.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research

Unlike global trends, US wind energy investment dropped to $3.8 billion in 1H25, the lowest since 2014. This decline aligns with a series of executive and legislative actions that have diminished the appeal for renewable energy investments in the country. Despite the overall decrease in US imports of wind equipment, imports from China remained relatively stable (might change with tariffs).

Imports of wind farm equipment from China to the US have decreased, but this has not prevented the former from trading with other countries. While China’s exports rose 30% YoY in 2Q25, those to the US fell 12% during the same period, reflecting the growing impact of tariffs, particularly in the wind equipment sector. However, the decline was offset by strong growth in China’s exports to Europe (notably the Netherlands, Spain and the UK), the Middle East and Africa (the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Nigeria), and Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines and Cambodia).

While the tonne-mile demand on US trade routes declined, it was largely counterbalanced by increased project cargo flows to Europe as well as emerging and established markets, maintaining healthy demand for Project Carriers.

The demand for clean technology and renewable energy projects continues to rise, despite current uncertainties. The need for clean energy, driven by the climate crisis and geopolitical issues, is prompting nations to prioritise energy security. This will likely increase the demand for Project Carriers, especially for wind farm equipment and other renewable and clean technology sources from 2025 to 2030.

Notes:1. Renewables include Offshore and Onshore Wind, Solar, Hydropower, Tidal, Wave, Geothermal, Biomass and Energy from Waste.2. Clean Tech includes Hydrogen, Carbon Capture and Energy Storage.Source: EIC,Drewry Maritime Research

Tariffs and policy changes in the US are creating an exceptional situation for the country where the demand for renewable energy is softening for the short term while it is increasing in Europe and other parts of the world. However, this situation also opens up opportunities for growth in Project Carriers over the next five-to-ten years. Although charter rates for Project Carriers may fluctuate over the short term due to US tariffs as well as interest rates and supply chain pressures, the long-term outlook for demand and charter rates in the project cargo sector remain promising.
