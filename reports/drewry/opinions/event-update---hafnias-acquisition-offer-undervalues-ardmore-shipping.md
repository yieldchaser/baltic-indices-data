# Event update - Hafnia’s acquisition offer undervalues Ardmore Shipping

*15 Jul 2020*

Ardmore Shipping (Ardmore) issued a press release on 6 July 2020 suggesting that Hafnia Limited (Hafnia) had approached it with an unsolicited acquisition offer on 19 June 2020. In an all-stock transaction, Hafnia planned to offer 2.4 shares of Hafnia for one share of Ardmore. This would translate into an implied price of USD 3.87 per share as per Ardmore. Ardmore has rejected the acquisition proposal, highlighting that the proposal substantially undervalued the company and was opportunistic. Using NAV valuation methodology, we believe Hafnia’s offer significantly undervalues Ardmore.

Currently, Hafnia is the second-largest owner of global product tanker fleet and the company’s fleet size would have increased to 110 vessels (from 85 vessels currently), had this acquisition gone through. A bigger fleet size may offer cost advantages with economies of scale and increased geographical diversification. Ardmore’s fleet has a slightly lower average age of 6.8 years compared to an average age of 7.5 years for Hafnia fleet. Furthermore, Hafnia has indicated that it expects a synergy of USD 15-20mn per year after this acquisition.

We expect product tanker freight rates to remain weak in the remaining months of 2020 due to the overall fall in demand, continuous fleet expansion and fewer demolitions. Global oil demand declined by 23.1% between December 2019 and May 2020 mainly on account of COVID-19. Furthermore, global refinery intake tumbled by 23.6% during the same period. According to the IEA, oil demand is expected to plunge 8.6 mbpd in 2020 compared with 2019, and refinery throughput will decline by 6.2 mbpd y/y in 2020.

Product tanker freight rates have started coming off after reaching a peak in April 2020. Vessels which were locked in floating storage earlier are returning to the trade. The Baltic Clean Tanker Index plummeted 32.5% in the last one month and 64.3% YTD as of 13 July 2020 (58.2% decline in the last three months).

Product tanker asset prices have started declining as freight rates have been dwindling. Asset prices for a five-year-old MR2 (51,000 dwt) have come off by about 8-10% in June with the recent fall in product tanker freight rates. Asset prices had gone up previously on higher demand expectations, driven by IMO 2020 regulations, which are now coming off.

Source:DMR,DMFR

While weaker product tanker prospects have weighed on both Hafnia and Ardmore share prices, Hafnia’s share price nosedived 40.6% YTD compared with 54.2% YTD decline in Ardmore share price. Ardmore’s share price has declined 18% (as of 13 July 2020) since 6 July 2020 when the company announced that it had rejected an acquisition offer from Hafnia, while the latter’s share price has remained almost flat during the same period. We believe the recent plunge in Ardmore’s share price is on account of investors being unhappy with Ardmore rejecting the acquisition offer. Ardmore’s poor profitability for a long time, lack of visibility in the present market and smaller scale of operations (compared to bigger players like Hafnia and Scorpio Tankers) are the main concerns of investors in our views.

In the last two months, Hafnia’s offer for acquiring Ardmore is the second news on consolidation in the product tanker space. Earlier, Norden announced a strategic product tanker partnership with Diamond S Shipping which has been named as DiaNor. Under this partnership, Diamond S will contribute 28 ships to Norient Product Pool, having a fleet of 150 product tankers.

Lower asset value and freight rates can be a potential catalyst for the consolidation as earnings of companies will be adversely affected. Smaller players with high borrowings may find it difficult to survive unless they go for sale of assets or sale and leaseback of vessels to boost their liquidity.

We believe Hafnia’s offer price significantly undervalues Ardmore (about 31.3% lower). We have arrived at a NAV based fair value of USD

5.64 per share for Ardmore. We calculate the NAV based on the fair market value of the fleet, overall debt and capital lease obligations, and after adjusting for other balance sheet items. We have valued Ardmore’s fleet based on current asset prices and have applied a 5% discount given negative product tanker prospects ahead.

Comparing the implied acquisition price of USD 3.87 per share with our NAV based fair value of USD 5.64 per share, we believe Hafnia’s implied acquisition price for Ardmore is about 31.3% below Ardmore’s fair value.
