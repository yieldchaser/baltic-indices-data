# Intra-Asia capacity grows, putting pressures on rates

*18 Jun 2025*

The huge intra-Asia container shipping market is regarded as relatively stable and robust, but will renewed capacity growth and softer demand change this?

Total effective shipping capacity deployed by carriers across all intra-Asian trades increased 8% between April and May, an increase of 646 kteu that is jeopardising the relative stability that characterises most of the intra-Asian trades, according to the latest update ofDrewry’s intra-Asia Container Market Insightonline tool.

The Top-5 intra-Asia routes alone saw carriers increase capacity by 9% between April and May, with double-digit growth on the routes Southeast Asia-Greater China and North Asia-Greater China and slower growth in the other routes – see below.

Over the same period, traffic volumes rose just 4% on these top 5 routes.

Of the main 3 trade routes that generate traffic volumes of more than 5 mteu yearly, the Greater China-to-North Asia trade had to absorb the largest increase in effective capacity in May. Carriers increased the effective capacity of this trade route to 595 kteu in May, by adding 41 kteu (+7.3%) compared to April. In the backhaul direction, the increase was even more pronounced as carriers added 83 kteu (+9.6%).

With Greater China-to-North Asia headhaul utilisation levels dropping from a relatively healthy 84% in April to just 74% in May, carriers, forwarders and shippers have started seeing spot rates falter.  Drewry expects freight rates to soften in the coming weeks.

The Southeast Asia-to-Greater China trade saw a monthly increase in capacity of 226 kteu (+15.4%). Once this capacity increase turns to the headhaul, probably next month, that could cause a cascade of freight rate reductions across other trade routes as well.

Drewry’sintra-Asia Container Index (composite), a weighted average of spot rates across 18 major trade routes in Asia, has declined 27% in the year to 1-15 Jun 25, to US$707/40ft container.
