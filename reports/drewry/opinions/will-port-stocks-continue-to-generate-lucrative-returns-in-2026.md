# Will Port stocks continue to generate lucrative returns in 2026?

*11 Dec 2025*

The Drewry Port Equity Index (DPEI) has delivered robust gains in the post-pandemic era (since 2021), growing at a CAGR of 18.5% since 2021 and has outperformed the S&P 500, which grew at 12.8% CAGR during the same period. More specifically, the sectorial index gained 16.0% in 2024 and has reported a 22.6% YTD return in 2025 (as of 5 December 2025). Higher global port throughput, supply chain disruptions and robust financial results have supported the index gain in the last two years. In this thought piece, we explore whether the port sector stocks can exhibit the robust performance of 2024 and 2025.

* YTD ending 05 December 2025.

Source:Drewry Maritime Financial Research

For companies comprising the DPEI that provide quarterly data, volumes rose by an average of 8.0% in 3Q25. Global Terminal operators (GTOs), with their broader outreach, outperformed the regionally focused Regional Terminal Operators (RTOs) by 4 percentage points. The volume growth supported the robust financial results. Cumulative topline and EBITDA increased 17.5% and 22.6%, respectively, translating into a higher margin of 50.0% (vs 3Q24: 47.9%). It is important to note that volumes include a mix of gross and consolidated volumes, depending on each company’s disclosure practices. Additionally, the sample for topline/EBITDA and for volumes differs, as a few companies release operational statistics but do not publish full financial results.

The aggregate index financials conceal the wide variations in individual companies’ financial performance. HHLA (Ports logistics division) posted a mid-single digit YoY growth in its topline (5.9% YoY) and EBITDA (7.8% YoY) in 3Q25. In contrast, ICTSI (Revenues - 9.7% YoY and EBITDA - 22.5% YoY), AD Ports’ ports cluster(Revenues - 18.2% YoY and EBITDA - 24.7% YoY) and Wesports (Revenues - 16.4% YoY and EBITDA - 22.6% YoY) all recorded double-digit increases. Even based on ROIC, ICTSI and Westports stand out among the other sampled port operators. Unsurprisingly, both companies have posted double-digit stock price gains since the start of the year.

Despite this notable index performance (refer to paragraph 1), the valuations do not look expensive (at least at the index level). The EV/ EBITDA of DPEI is currently trading at approximately 10% discount compared to its long-term average of 10.7x (since 2018). We believe this discount showcases investors’ caution towards the uncertain trade policies and a volatile geopolitical landscape, which has kept them away from paying peak multiples to port equities.

Even though the DPEI has outperformed broader markets in 2025, heading into 2026 the situation is meaningfully different. A complex mix of macro-economic situations and higher base effect for the industry volumes in a stable global GDP growth environment (2026: 3.1% YoY vs 2025: +3.2% YoY, Oxford Economics) hints towards a slowdown in the world container throughput growth (2026: 1.5% YoY vs 2025: 5.2% YoY). In such an environment, we call for a stock-specific approach, rather than a broad index-based long position. We prefer companies with diversified presence, stable traffic bases and consistently high ROIC.
