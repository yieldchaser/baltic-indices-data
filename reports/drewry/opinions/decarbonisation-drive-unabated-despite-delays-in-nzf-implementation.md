# Decarbonisation drive unabated despite delays in NZF implementation

*04 Dec 2025*

The IMO’s efforts to develop mid-term measures through the ‘Net-zero Framework’ did not proceed as anticipated. A majority vote in favour of a “one-year delay” for deciding whether or not to adopt the framework is being viewed by many as something that can undermine the framework altogether. While the picture might seem dismal, it does not signal the end of the shipping sector’s decarbonisation journey.

Although the decision to adopt the ‘Net-zero Framework’ has been postponed by a year, the IMO’s overall GHG strategy (2023) remains in place, and decisions will continue to consider the GHG emission-reduction targets outlined in the strategy.

Existing short-term measures, including the CII regulation, will continue with timely reviews and are strengthened as needed, with the recent updates confirming the CII reduction factors up to 2030 (21.5% reduction in 2030).

The EU has been at the forefront of the shipping industry’s decarbonisation efforts and has already taken several concrete steps in this direction. From the inclusion of ships calling at EU ports in the existing Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) to building a new framework called FuelEU Maritime, which accounts for the life-cycle emissions of fuels used on board, the EU is charting its own path and demonstrating its commitment through strict implementation.

Post the adjournment, there are a few developments which give a clear indication that the decarbonisation journey is continuing.

The delay in adopting the Net-zero Framework (NZF) has undoubtedly created uncertainty, leaving several strategic decisions in the lurch, particularly for smaller shipowners that were awaiting regulatory clarity to guide their investments. Nevertheless, many stakeholders with internal decarbonisation targets continue to advance their transition plans, supported by emerging financial mechanisms such as “Green/Transition Finance framework” and the “Fund for Energy Efficiency Technologies (FEET)”. In parallel, regional initiatives, most notably within the EU, are compelling vessels calling at their ports to comply with decarbonisation measures, generating valuable insights and data that can strengthen future IMO policymaking.

Collectively, these developments demonstrate that the global shipping industry remains firmly committed to its decarbonisation journey. While the delay in NZF adoption presents a temporary hurdle, the eventual establishment of a unified global framework will be essential for aligning efforts, sustaining momentum and paving a clear path towards a greener maritime future.
