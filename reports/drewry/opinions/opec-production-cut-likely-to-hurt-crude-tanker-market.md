# OPEC+ production cut likely to hurt crude tanker market

*01 Dec 2023*

Shipping rates are likely to soften as production cuts deepen in an already weakened demand, signalling further turmoil in the tanker market.

On 30 November, major oil-producing countries agreed to curtail oil production by nearly 2.2 million barrels per day (mbpd) starting in 1Q24, including an existing 1.0 mbpd cut by Saudi Arabia and 0.3 mbpd cut by Russia. This concerted effort aims to stabilise and boost oil prices amid economic uncertainties and heightened production from competitors.

Saudi Arabia extended its voluntary production cut of 1.0 mbpd, a measure initiated in July. Additionally, Russia announced a 200,000 barrel per day (bpd) cut in exports of refined products, while also holding back 300,000 bpd of crude oil exports.

The cartel is strategically preventing excessive inventory buildups and averting prices from plummeting below the current levels. Brazil, a significant non-member oil producer, is expected to join OPEC+ in 2024, which could further bolster the cartel’s position. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), Brazil is projected to contribute about 3.8 mbpd of crude oil in 2024.

However, challenges loom on the horizon as global oil demand is likely to slump in 2024, driven by an economic slowdown in China- the largest oil importer- and lacklustre growth prospects for the global economy.

As per IEA’s previous forecast, the market was expected to be in surplus in 1Q24 due to expected economic deceleration. After the cartel decided to slash production for 1Q24, the market will be in deficit. However, rates are likely to soften in 1Q24 despite deficit oil market, seasonal weak demand and production cuts (which could lead to an inventory drawdown), assuming a status quo situation.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research, IEA

If we translate production cuts into vessel demand, it will roughly result in 15-16 VLCC cargo load equivalents monthly in 1Q24. If OPEC+ successfully manages to support prices, in all likelihood, the deficit cargo will be drawn from the inventory. As a result, day rate earnings will be under pressure, which is already staring at a receding demand due to economic volatility. However, it is difficult to quantify the exact impact. Not only is bleak demand pushing oil producers to take corrective measures, but it is also keeping shipowners on their toes.
