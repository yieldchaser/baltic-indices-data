# Surging demand and increasing bunker cost to push up domestic chemical freight rates in China

*11 Mar 2019*

China’s domestic demand for chemical shipping has been boosted by the rapid expansion in Chinese base chemical production capacity for products such as paraxylene and stryrene.

Some 55% of the new production capacity is located in East China, 23% in North China and 22% in South China. Given that domestic supply of major chemical products is increasing, demand for vessels for the country’s domestic chemical trade is set to increase, strengthening coastal freight rates. In fact, Chinese coastal chemical trade increased by 7% in 2018 year on year, up from a 3% rise in 2017. However, the flip side is that imports of paraxylene, styrene and other base chemicals by China from other Asian suppliers are likely to fall.

Meanwhile, as the Chinese government controls the development of the domestic chemical fleet, shipowners have to apply for licenses to operate within the country. In 2018, the government approved more additional licenses for the domestic fleet due to the surge in domestic demand. Nineteen new China-flagged chemical tankers were delivered in 2018; while three vessels were withdrawn from the chemical shipping market.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research

Looking ahead we expect the Chinese-flagged chemical tanker fleet to expanding further to meet rising domestic shipments of base and semi-finished liquid chemicals. However, despite an increase in vessel supply, freight rates on China’s coastal routes will strengthen in 2019 and 2020 on the back of a stronger domestic demand and higher bunker cost.

In 2018, average freight rates on domestic China routes increased 2-3% compared with 2017, and similar increases are likely to occur in 2019 and 2020. An important factor that will underpin the continued rise in domestic chemical tanker freight rates is that China has already extended its 0.5% bunker fuel sulphur limit from the initially designated Emission Control Areas (ECAs) to the entire Chinese coastline from 1 January 2019.
