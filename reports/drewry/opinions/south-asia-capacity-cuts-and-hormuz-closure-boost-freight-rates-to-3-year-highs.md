# South Asia capacity cuts and Hormuz closure boost freight rates to 3-year highs

*23 Jul 2026*

Why cost inflation faced by shippers is spreading – rather than easing.

Freight rates from South Asia to the US and Europe have surged about 50% in the last two weeks, while rates to the UAE and Saudi Arabia have remained more than three times historical levels for the past three months as tensions surrounding the Iran conflict and the Strait of Hormuz disrupted regional shipping, according to Drewry’sContainer Freight Rate Insight.

The Middle East trades have experienced the steepest increases, with rising demand, congestion at key Indian ports, tightening vessel supply, and new carrier surcharges announced for July adding further upward pressure.

JNPT–to-Jebel Ali spot freight rates, which averaged around $734 per 40ft container throughout 2025, exploded to $6,216 in April 2026 following the escalation of the Iran conflict — an 807% increase in two months. Shipments to Jebel Ali now use an overland route which bypasses the Strait of Hormuz, adding cost and transit time. Rates have since partially corrected to $5,609 in June but remain at roughly eight times their pre-crisis level. JNPT–to-Jeddah rates followed a similar trajectory, rising from $1,484 in February 2026 to $5,354 in March before easing slightly to $4,598 in June. The severity of the spike reflects a near-complete disruption to direct Gulf services, forcing cargo onto longer, costlier routing options.

JNPT–Genoa rates rose 54% to $5,030 per 40ft, while JNPT–New York jumped 43% to $5,797 per 40ft — the highest level since Sep-2024, triggered by a sharp tightening in supply. MSC's withdrew its Indus Express service and ONE's WIN service operating just a single sailing in June removed significant capacity from the market, while export demand has rebounded strongly.

It means that ex-India services to the US East Coast and North Europe are now sold out through late August, with carriers reporting booking requests at more than double normal weekly levels. Carriers have responded by tightening booking conditions — Hapag-Lloyd tripled cancellation penalties to $300 per container, and Maersk, MSC, Cosco and ONE raised fee to $100–$200 per container from the South Asia trade.

Source:Drewry Container Freight Rate Insight

A period of overcapacity on South Asia's trade lanes is rapidly giving way to tighter conditions. Deployed capacity on the South Asia–Med route contracted 6% Month-on-Month in June, while on the South Asia-US route the surplus has been swiftly unwound — the exit of ONE's WIN service (stripping Cosco and HMM of slot rights) alongside MSC's Indus Express suspension has cut capacity by around 14% in two months.

Drewry’s industry sources confirm that cargo rollovers on certain services at Nhava Sheva and Mundra have hit 2,000–3,000 TEUs per sailing, pushing carriers such as CMA CGM to deploy extra-loaders; MSC is set to reinstate the Indus Express from late August. Port congestion is compounding the pressure: vessel waiting times at JNPT jumped from 18 hours in Week 27 to 26 hours in Week 28, adding cost and transit uncertainty for exporters across the region.

Source:Drewry Container Capacity Insight

It means that shippers and forwarders sourcing capacity from South Asia face a materially tighter and costlier market.

Drewry expects freight rates from South Asia trades to strengthen further as announced surcharges take effect and space remains constrained. On the route to the Middle East, rates are likely to remain highly elevated for as long as the Iran conflict persists and direct Gulf services remain disrupted, while the threat of Houthis attacks on ships sailing to Jeddah and other Saudi ports could further disrupt shipping operations.

The outbreak of war grounded an estimated 13–15% of global air capacity as major Gulf carriers curtailed operations. While services gradually resumed, the impact extended beyond the region, with airfreight rates from South Asia also showing an immediate impact. Spot freight rates from Delhi and Mumbai to London increased between 57% and 69% between February and June.
