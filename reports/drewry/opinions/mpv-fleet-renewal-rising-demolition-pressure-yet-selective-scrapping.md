# MPV fleet renewal: Rising demolition pressure, yet selective scrapping

*26 Aug 2026*

Stricter regulations, older fleet and changing cargo needs may lead to large-scale scrapping, but that has not been the case in the General Cargo sector. Instead, the focus has shifted to building more efficient and versatile vessels with scrapping becoming selective and solely dependent on the commercial viability of the vessels.

The General Cargo segment has been evolving with new environmental regulations, diverse cargo types and volatile earnings reshaping the industry.

In this analysis, we focus on the modelling assumptions to arrive at probable demolition candidates over the next 12 months by considering vessel inactivity, vessel age and special survey due dates. We will identify the segments that face greater replacement pressure and assess the implications for fleet makeup, market balance and investment trends.

The 5–10k dwt segment is the hardest hit, with the highest likelihood of increased demolition over the next 12 months. In contrast, 10–20k dwt vessels face the lowest demolition risk, as they are the youngest among the size ranges, with an average age of around 15 years.

Our findings indicate that vessels aged 25 years and above face a higher likelihood of demolition, although their continued operation ultimately depends on earnings, rising compliance costs, and the availability of employment opportunities.

When freight markets are strong, vessels tend to stay in service for longer, while in weaker markets, older ships face greater pressure, but they could still be redeployed, laid up or sold to secondary markets rather than being scrapped right away. As the current market is stable, we observe a trend towards fleet modernisation due to stringent environmental regulations.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research

Inactivity in older vessels and their special survey needs indicate the part of the fleet that is under commercial pressure. However, it is important to note the difference between the factors that could lead these vessels to the scrapyards:

Many vessels that are 20–25 years old or more are still in use as they are debt-free, relatively inexpensive to operate, serve special markets and can be used for various cargo types. As a result, we can safely say that vessel age alone is not the only deciding factor for scrapping in the General cargo segment.

Drewry believes the General Cargo fleet will evolve mostly due to ongoing economic and regulatory pressures, not rapid scrapping, which remains a minor factor. Fleet renewal will be gradual as many older vessels remain commercially viable due to their flexibility and low capital intensity. Older ships will still be used in regional trades and lower-profit areas, creating a mixed fleet.

The result is a measured transition marked by selective investment, improving efficiency and a broadly stable supply–demand balance, with 1.93 mdwt expected to be demolished over the next 12 months.
