# Surprise production curb by OPEC+ members to derail the crude tanker market?

*04 Apr 2023*

The recent decision by some OPEC+ members to voluntarily cut oil production threatens to cool off the heated crude tanker market if the production curbs materialise.

The pre-emptive action by the cartel to cope with the likely weakness in demand will squeeze global oil supply, hurting tonnage demand in the crude tanker market. However, if the cartel keeps the oil market well supplied, the tanker market will continue to enjoy attractive earnings this year.

Source:Drewry Maritime Research

Nine OPEC+ members, including Saudi Arabia and Russia, have decided to cut production by 1.6 mbpd from May to December 2023 to stabilise the oil market. With this, the overall OPEC+ production cut will reach 3.66 mbpd, including 2 mbpd cut in October 2022, significantly squeezing oil supply in the market. The shocking decision by Saudi Arabia and its allies came after the crude oil prices plunged to $70 per barrel in late March as the recent collapse of Silicon Valley Bank in the US raised concerns over the global economic recession.

Although the global oil demand outlook is highly uncertain at this stage, the crude tanker market might feel the heat of weak demand irrespective of the trajectory of the forward oil demand curve if OPEC+ producers keep the crude oil output at the recently agreed levels.

If the global oil demand falters in line with the expectations of the OPEC+ producers, the oil market will be balanced, but lower demand will squeeze refinery runs and crude oil trade, hurting tanker rates. Similarly, if oil demand remains strong in line with the IEA’s forecasts, the production curbs by OPEC+ will lead to a massive drawdown in strategic and commercial inventories, hampering crude oil trade and crude tanker demand.

According to the IEA, global oil demand is expected to increase by 2 mbpd in 2023, supported by a rebound in Chinese demand. Although the expected resurgence in China’s oil demand in 2023 bodes well for the crude tanker market, especially for VLCCs, truncated OPEC+ supply can hamper the demand prospects.

Even before the voluntary production cut by Saudi Arabia and its allies on 4 April, the oil market was expected to remain in a significant deficit in 2H23 on account of the 2 mbpd of production cut by OPEC+ and 0.5 mbpd voluntary production cut by Russia. The oil market deficit will widen further with the recent voluntary curbs, which could potentially crush crude tanker demand.

Nonetheless, we believe the recent move by the OPEC+ members is purely aimed at supporting the oil prices, and the cartel will bring back the output once demand improves. If OPEC+ keeps the oil market balanced, a likely surge in oil demand in 2H23 on the back of the resurgent Chinese demand will boost crude oil trade. Although some softening in crude tanker earnings in the short term cannot be ruled out, firmness in crude oil trade in tandem with the new trade patterns, will keep the sheen of the crude tanker market intact in 2H23.
