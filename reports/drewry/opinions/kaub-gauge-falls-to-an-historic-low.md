# Kaub gauge falls to an historic low

*19 Aug 2026*

The Rhine’s Kaub gauge fell to an historic low of 6cm in August, a level that effectively cuts the vital European logistics waterway in two.

To illustrate the scale of the issue, Commerzbank has estimated a hit of 0.35 percentage points to German GDP if critically low water persists through mid-September.

In June, Rhine gauges were reduced but still navigable. By late-July the Kaub gauge (a key water gauge at the Rhine chokepoint of Kaub) fell under 40cm, the threshold where barge logistics becomes less economically viable, and barge laden weights shrink to 20% of normal capacity.

With barges able to carry only a fraction of their normal load, the outflow of import containers from Rotterdam, Antwerp and the Hamburg range is severely throttled.

In July, with the water levels across Europe’s navigable waterways (including the Rhine, Elbe and Danube) critical, but not catastrophic, Hamburg’s CTA terminal was already running at 89% yard utilisation with average pre-berth vessel waiting around 0.3 days.

By late-August the Kaub gauge fell to a historic low of 6cm, a level that effectively splits the river in two: barges already upstream can no longer return to the Rotterdam-Antwerp range, and vice-versa.

This results in an effective capacity loss for the deep-sea terminals that depend on the river as an evacuation route.

With roughly 35% of containers moving between Rotterdam and its hinterland normally traveling by barge, these import boxes must depart via a road and rail network that is already fully utilised.

This means rising yard occupancy as boxes destined for the Rhine corridor back up on the quay with no easy alternative. Carriers may layer-on further surcharges or potentially blank or restructure calls if congestion deepens.

In July, average pre-berth waiting time at Rotterdam was already elevated at 0.5 days, while at Antwerp it surged to 1.5 days (due to unrelated issues). We expect the true impact of the drought to be apparent in the August and September data.

A similar situation has developed on the Danube, where record low water levels along its entire length (as measured by gauges in Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Serbia, Romania and Ukraine) has exposed so-called ‘hunger-stones’ and is severely restricting upstream transport. Constrained by shallow stretches and reduced draft limits, barges are being forced to carry substantially smaller loads, reduced to just 30% of the normal capacity, leaving cargo stranded at the port of Constanta.

The low water level has had myriad impacts, including a massive cut in power generation from hydroelectric power stations as well as nuclear power plants in Serbia, Hungary and Romania, with estimates of the nuclear power generation shortfall in the region reaching 4 gigawatts.

To illustrate the scale of the issue, Commerzbank has estimated a hit to German GDP of 0.35 percentage points if critically low water persists through mid-September.
