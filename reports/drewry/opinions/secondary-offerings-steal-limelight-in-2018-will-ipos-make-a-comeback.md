# Secondary offerings steal limelight in 2018; but will IPOs make a comeback?

*27 Mar 2019*

Despite low levels of interest in shipping IPOs in the US over the last few years, listed shipping companies continue to raise money through secondary/additional offerings and bond offerings in the US market. In addition, Oslo is slowly emerging as the preferred platform for shipping companies to raise equity. With the financial performance of shipping companies gradually improving, the woes of investors with respect to negative returns and irregular dividend streams should in our view be addressed. Accordingly, we expect more shipping IPOs globally from 2H19.

The amount and frequency of dividend payments are slated to increase for shipping companies engaged in LNG, crude and product, and dry bulk trades with the expectation of positive earnings growth.

In the LNG shipping space, Gaslog LNG and Golar LNG reported higher earnings in 2018 on account of the buoyant spot market. Firm LNG trade will further help LNG shipping companies to post better results in 2019. Robust growth of 1.4 mbpd in global oil demand and higher refinery runs ahead of the upcoming IMO sulphur regulation will keep the crude trade firm in 2019, and thus, will strengthen earnings of crude tankers in 2H19. Additionally, continued rapid growth in US crude exports will further boost tonne-mile crude oil demand during the year. Within the dry bulk segment, Star Bulk Carriers and Golden Ocean returned to profitability in 2018. Golden Ocean paid out USD 64.9m in dividends in 2018-19, transferring value to shareholders and reinforcing confidence in the market recovery. Currently, Capesizes are underperforming due to macro-level uncertainties highlighted above and the collapse of the Vale dam, but we expect conditions to improve and earnings to bottom out gradually.

Brighter prospects are also among the core reasons why secondary offerings across segments are gaining foothold and investors are re-visiting investment in the shipping industry. Undeniably, US securities markets provide greater access to liquidity and visibility, and active trading of equity instruments. As shipping companies increase profitability, investors will gradually regain confidence, paving the way for new companies to secure support while launching IPOs.
