# Textainer to buy SeaCo

*03 Jun 2025*

Textainer’s decision to buy SeaCo will herald in a new phase of consolidation in the maritime container leasing industry. It follows Triton International’s acquisition of Global Container International earlier this year and it means the top two lessors will control more than 50% of leased assets.

Textainer, which was acquired by US investment firm Stonepeak in March 2024, first divulged its interest in SeaCo - owned by the Chinese-controlled transport and property conglomerate HNA Group - in June 2024 with a US$5 billion price tag being talked about. In May 2025, following months of detailed due-diligence, a final agreement was signed between Typewriter Ascend Ltd, which is an entity controlled by Stonepeak and an affiliate of Textainer, and Bohai Leasing Co Ltd, owned by HNA, for the purchase of Global Sea Containers Ltd (Seaco), but for an equity purchase price of just US$1.75 billion. It is also understood that the final price will be subject to what Stonepeak referred to as “certain adjustments”.

Currently SeaCo is the world’s fifth largest lessor of maritime containers, controlling a portfolio of 2.4 mteu, of which 17% (over 412,000 teu) are reefer containers. It is also the largest lessor of special dry freight containers and owns a substantial inventory of tank containers (about 39,000 teu). The company operates 23 offices and 360 depots across the world.

Textainer is the second largest lessor in the world with just under 7 mteu under its control. Approximately 95% of which are standard dry freight units. While in teu/unit terms the acquisition of SeaCo will diversify its fleet and broaden its container offering, it will not quite push Textainer back to the top of the leasing league, a position it lost out on in 2025 when Triton Container International and the TAL Intermodal Group merged.

Triton will remain the leading lessor of containers with a fleet just in excess of 7.5 mteu. This assumes that its more than US$1 billion acquisition of GCI announced earlier this year will be approved by the regulators. However, the large number of special containers in SeaCo’s fleet means that on a ceu basis, Textainer’s pool will be the most valuable in the leasing sector and worth almost 4% more than Triton’s (see Table).

Given that another large lessor is being taken over by a competitor, regulatory authorities in China, the US and Europe, which will need to approve the deal, are likely to raise some concerns over concentration and market dominance issues. The deal will, for instance, result in just five companies owning over 88% (on a teu basis) of the total leased pool of equipment in the fleet. At the end of 2024, Drewry research revealed that lessors controlled 48.1% of all containers in operation.
