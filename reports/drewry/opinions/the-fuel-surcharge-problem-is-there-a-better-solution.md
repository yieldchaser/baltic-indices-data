# The Fuel Surcharge Problem: Is There a Better Solution?

*09 Jun 2026*

Fuel surcharges have become one of the most challenging and unpredictable components of ocean freight costs for shippers. Cargo owners are often faced with significant variations in surcharge levels between carriers operating on the same trade lane, with differences of more than $200 per 40’ dry container observed between the highest and lowest BAF values in the same quarter.

Such variation creates difficulties for cargo owners and procurement teams, complicating carrier comparisons and increasing the risk of unexpected transportation costs.

More recently, with escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, ocean carriers introduced the Emergency Bunker Adjustment Factors (eBAFs) in addition to existing BAF charges, which adds a further layer of complexity - as well as significantly increasing overall surcharge costs.

Beyond the financial impact, the lack of standardisation creates a considerable administrative burden for shippers. Procurement, logistics and finance teams must verify that surcharges have been applied correctly, reconcile changing BAF levels against contractual agreements, and investigate billing discrepancies. This increases the time and effort required for freight auditing and invoice validation, while also raising the risk of errors and disputes.

While no single approach can eliminate all of these challenges, a standardised and transparent fuel surcharge policy can help minimise many of them.Drewry’s Standard BAF Policywas developed to provide a market-aligned, independently calculated mechanism for fuel cost recovery that can be applied consistently across all service providers. By using a common methodology across trades and carriers, it improves transparency, simplifies carrier comparisons, and provides greater predictability of fuel-related costs.

Unlike carrier-defined BAFs,Drewry’s BAFvalues followed a clear and consistent pattern throughout the year, moving in line with changes in underlying fuel market conditions. Quarterly adjustments were applied using the same methodology across all trades, ensuring that surcharge movements remained transparent, predictable and fully aligned with fuel price developments. This consistency allowed shippers to better understand and anticipate fuel-related costs while avoiding the complexity associated with different carrier BAF values.
