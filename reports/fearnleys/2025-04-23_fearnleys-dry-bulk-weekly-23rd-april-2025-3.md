## Capesize/Newcastlemax

The market rallied around this time of year both in 2023 and 2024. China's imported iron ore consumption is up 4.5% month-on-month, a level that ranks amongst the highest seen in the last three years. This strongly suggests the market will rise in the coming weeks. 
Looking further ahead, the market could soften in June as the VLOCs will be back to take Brazil cargoes. Further, there are indications that China's iron ore consumption will drop over the next few months as we are in the peak season, and as Trump's trade war has hurt sentiment and the export sector.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax 

The count of ballasters heading to Indonesia has rebounded since the March low. However, further upside could be limited as the number is now on par with 2022 and 2023 levels. The levels of last year are not likely to be reached, primarily because of the high inventories in China. 
We expect a stable to slightly rising market until late May or early June, as by then the ECSAM grain season should wind down, and downside risks increase. Further, Canada and US grains volumes have been higher this year than both 2023 and 2024, but seasonality means these volumes will trend down over the next months.

## Supramax/Ultramax 

A little over one month ago, we wrote: 

*"Around this time of year, the market usually settles and then trends sideways in Q2. We find it likely that the BSI-63 index will settle in the low teens.
The correlation to the average seasonal movement suggested the market would be a bit higher now. The market could still climb slightly due to ECSAM grains and a slightly positive indication from industrial metals prices and manufacturing PMIs. However, fundamentals do not seem supportive of an index climb much higher than 13.000 USD pd."*

Since then, the index has stayed below 13kpd. We do not see much reason to expect the market to go much higher than that over the next month. The Pacific is quite firm, but signs of weakness are appearing in the Atlantic. The macroeconomic backdrop is not exactly supportive these days either.
