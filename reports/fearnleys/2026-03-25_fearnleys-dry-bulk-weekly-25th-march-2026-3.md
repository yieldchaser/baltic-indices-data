## Capesize/Newcastlemax

The market still seems most likely to rise over the next few weeks. 

**Top left chart:** Blast furnace output has rebounded after some weeks of maintenance. Steel mill profitability does not suggest much further upside, however. Port inventory draws in China is a downside risk now, as MySteel's index of seaborne iron ore prices is higher than their index of prices of iron ore in ports. 
**Top right chart:** Shipment volumes were 8.7% higher than last year through week 12. 
**Bottom left chart:** The South Atlantic tightness indicator has remained high for two months, but the market has yet to muster a real rally. There is still potential for that, however, which is further supported by high congestion of VLOCs in Brazil. 
**Bottom right chart:** The laden/ballast ratio is above average, but far from an extreme. It must rise further before one eventually can say the market is in toppish territory.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax 

The below indicators still suggest that the market will most likely continue trending down over the next few weeks. 

**Top left chart:** Shipment volumes were up 5.5% year on year through week 12. 
**Top right chart:** Contango in the coal futures curve increased further over the last week, which is surprising given the circumstances. This suggests P5 will continue the softening trend going forward. 
**Bottom left chart:** The Pacific tightness indicator still suggests weakness going forward. The indicator has stopped falling, however, which might mean the market will find a bottom in early/mid April. 
**Bottom right chart:** The SATL tightness indicator has continued to fall, suggesting further downside for P6.

## Supramax/Ultramax 

Signs of market stabilization, as the ballast/laden vessel ratio has dropped. However, we dont see much reason for the market to rally. 

**Top left chart:** The three-month change in the price of copper has fallen noticeably. Usually, there is a lag of a few months before the same development is seen in the Supramax/Ultramax market. The correlation is still not suggesting the market will fall significantly, but rather that the upside is highly limited going forward. 
**Top right chart:** The chart compares the ballaster/laden vessel ratio against the S11TC index. A low ballaster/laden vessel ratio reflects a tighter market balance. 
**Bottom left chart:** Industrial metals prices have displayed weakness in the last couple of months. We held the view before this year that 1-year TC rates likely would exceed the peak reached in 2024. This now seems much less likely, as fundamentals have taken a hit from the consequences of the Iran war. 
**Bottom right chart:** Shipment volumes were up 8.9% year on year through week 12.

## Handysize

The market could soon find its footing, as there are signs that the Ultramax market is stabilising. 

**Top left chart:** The ratio between Rio-Recalada / Skaw-Passero (HS3) and SEA / Singapore-Japan (HS5) is usually a good predictor of tops and bottoms of the HS3 route. A higher ratio suggests a market top, and a lower ratio suggests a market bottom. 
**Top right chart:** Through week 11, Handysize shipment volumes were 6.0% higher than the same period in 2025. 
**Bottom left chart:** The correlation between the S11TC and HS7TC is over 90%. The correlation is even higher when the S11TC index is shifted forward a few days, which means that movements in the S11TC index often lead the movements of the HS7TC. Further downside in the Handysize index is likely over the next week. 
**Bottom right chart:** The changes in the price of copper usually lead the changes in second-hand values, by about 6 months. Positive momentum for values is still suggested for the coming months, though also a potential peak in the middle of this year.
