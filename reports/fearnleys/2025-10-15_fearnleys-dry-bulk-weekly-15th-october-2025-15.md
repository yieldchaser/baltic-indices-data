## Capesize/Newcastlemax

Last week we wrote "*No data from China last week due to holidays. The key datapoint to follow is imported iron ore consumption, which has been stable at around 3 million tons per day since the start of May. Total iron ore inventories have barely changed since May as well. Prices have seen more significant swings, but as long as volumes are not impacted, it will not mean much for the Capesize market. The narrowing spread of near-month iron ore futures is something we have pointed to for a while that might lead to a weaker Q4 than Q3 (top right chart). Interestingly, the futures curve has turned more backwardated since the BHP news broke, so that indicator is now suggesting Q1 market strength following a weaker Q4. Otherwise, there has been an unusual development in price spreads between various iron ore grades. Australian iron ore has weakened compared to lower grades (usually a bearish sign), but higher-grade Brazilian ores have increased in price relative to Australian ores.*" 

Wild week behind us, but China's exemptions to self-built ships cooled things off, and it seems the market impact will be minimal. 
Otherwise, there is no significant change to what we wrote last week. Iron ore demand in China remains stable, as does total iron ore inventories. Bauxite shipments keeps making seasonally adjusted all-time highs. The most interesting data point regarding the near-term is that the ballaster/laden vessel ratio is relatively high, which could cause a correction.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax 

Last week we wrote: *"The South Atlantic seems to stabilize, so a rebound could be underway. The North-Atlantic looks a bit over-tonnaged, however, so that is where the main downside risk lies. The Ultramax market has started to weaken there. 
In the Pacific, the market looks resilient, and is suggested to remain so by the coal futures curve. Stable to firm outlook near-term for P5TC."*

The South Atlantic vessel tightness indicator suggests a peak around the October/November month shift. This could align with a peak in the coal futures curve lead, as that is also peaking around the month shift. The North Atlantic vessel tightness indicator is neutral, having turned a bit more bullish since last week. However, a US-China soybean deal seems out of the question now.

## Supramax/Ultramax 

Last week, we wrote: *"Sharp correction on several Atlantic routes in the last days. The S4A index could fall to the low 20.000s pd, as that would normalise the spread over Pacific rounds. We doubt Pacific indices will correct much further, as earnings are now similar to Kamsarmaxes, with the outlook there being firm/stable."*

Pacific routes are likely to be stable to firm in the near-term, whereas downside risk in the Atlantic remains. As predicted by the price change of copper, market momentum has peaked, so new 11TC/10TC index highs are unlikely before year-end, basis the 3 months lead shown on the top left chart.
