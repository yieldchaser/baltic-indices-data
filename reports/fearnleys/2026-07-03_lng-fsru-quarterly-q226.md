## Market reflections: Sticky FSRU Fingers

Close to 100 days into what is now firmly one of the most significant — and least anticipated — LNG supply disruptions on record, we draw the conclusion that **it is supply that is the problem, not demand**. We claim no prize for stating the obvious, but it is reassuring to see the underlying drivers of the FSRU market remaining robust. As we entered the quarter we saw a prompt reaction from ongoing projects to pause activities, but the **prevailing message in our engagement with projects and end-users since is that they need to move now to be ready for 2028–2030**. "That is when the supply wave emerges, now that Middle East start-ups have been pushed out" has been playing on repeat in our discussions.

Despite the near-term uncertainty — or at least the increased risk premium — created by the situation in the Middle East, there has not yet been a sense of urgency around **long-term supply diversification** to the same extent seen in the wake of Russia-Ukraine in 2022. **LNG SPA contracting data reflects relatively muted activity so far in 2026**, with around 30 MTPA contracted year-to-date. We attribute this to a perception that the situation will eventually normalise, as opposed to the view that the disruption of Russian gas flows to Europe would create a permanent structural gap. However, contracts entered into this year appear to be at lower pricing points — whether oil-linked, gas hub or hybrid structures — **suggesting suppliers remain eager to place volumes**. In the context of FSRUs, **a 12%-to-Brent deal is considerably more stimulating than the 14–16%** seen in 2023–2024, and lower-cost SPAs broadly improve project economics in ways that strengthen the case for FSRU-based solutions.

In terms of **project activity**, we see a relatively constant balance between **Asia and LatAm, with Europe rounding off the geographical focus**. Interest from both private developers and public entities remains strong — a signal that demand is being driven by commercial opportunity and energy security in equal measure. What is worth noting is that the **"canary in the coalmine"** we thought we saw earlier — with suppliers looking to push into FSRU and downstream projects — **has not been visible recently**, which we read as a moderately constructive signal for market discipline.

Chartering activity was higher during the quarter, with **one newbuild booked back-to-back with a 20-year project** at what appears to be a **time charter of around US$125,000–130,000/day**, and one contract substitution in **Colombia for a 7-year deal rumoured to be in the range of US$150,000/day**. We expect to see a few further deals — assuming conditions precedent are lifted — in H2 2026 at somewhat lower levels based on conversions, but still at what can only be described as very healthy returns of around **6–8x Capex/EBITDA**.

Based on our assessment of the market, the **near-to-mid-term outlook for FSRUs has improved through the quarter**. Visibility on a number of "may become available" assets looking to extend or relocate has effectively left the market sold out of prompt tonnage — without forward commitments — until the latter part of 2027. Speculative contracting activity across both newbuilds and conversions has slowed, or more precisely, units that were sitting semi-speculatively have now found contracts. **FSRU providers have also developed the discipline to hold off** on committing tonnage until all conditions precedent are lifted, which is currently causing a handful of "ready-to-go" conversions to be delayed.

To summarize: **elevated FSRU rate levels have proven stickier than we anticipated 12 months ago**. While project development timelines still tend to follow a sine curve rather than a straight line, the discipline among FSRU providers around speculative ordering continues — for now — to **hold back the typical supply wave**. With near-term available FSRUs being absorbed into the market, partly at the expense of conversions and newbuilds, **the ingredients are in place for a solid second half of the year.**

### Quarterly corporate highlights: Nothing to knock your socks off...or?

**NFEs** restructuring process has gone through the motions in Q2 with press releases generally focused on legal aspects and the approval of the UK restructuring plan. In light of being in a restructuring process there have been no updates or quarterly releases, but we are hearing talks of their TM2500s being for sale and the other operations (Mexico, FastLNG1 and Puerto Rico) operating relatively steadily.

**Excelerate** reported Q1/26 in early-May with the most prominent news being the **EXCELERATE ACADIA** heading for a 9-month charter for NEPCO in Aqaba, Jordan as NEPCO are still not ready with their onshore solution which was due to start in Q3/26. The indicated EBITDA for the NEPCO-charter is ~US$ 20m in 2026 (which would equate to a bareboat rate of US$ 110,000/day assuming 01st July to 31st Dec 2026). Excelerate are not expecting to generate much income from the Petrobangla supply agreement due to FM from Qatar Energy – a situation all too familiar for many Asian buyers. The upcoming conversion of **EXCELERATE SHENANDOAH** is progressing with LoI signed with Seatrium for the conversion works. While still not allocated to a project, we consider the India-project at Haldia a potentially suitable fit. The most bullish data point from an FSRU perspective was Excelerate’s comment on expecting to redeploy **EXPRESS** at improved economic terms. We now have visibility on this likely being in Colombia to Frontera Energy, which likely justifies the higher charter rate in light of the substantially higher credit risk compared to ADNOC. 

Following the bond issuance in late-2025, **Kinetics** is now also in the public domain with a recent update on the progress for conversion of their three FSRUs. **OCEANIA** has been completed in Q2 with **TÜRKIYE** targeted for completion in Q3/26 before their largest FSRU; **KARADENIZ** arrives in Q4/27. We see the two initial vessels targeted for Karpowership’s fleet of powerships, but the last FSRU has potential to compete in the “standard” FSRU market given a very wide range of operating conditions.

Finally, perhaps the most interesting corporate movement are those that should be considered as “no smoke without fire” which suggest there may be some upcoming transaction in the coming months that could slightly alter the market balance. More about that when/if it happens.

### Quarterly focus: Understand FSRU demand to gain the upper hand and execute as planned

So far in 2026, the FSRU market has kept up a decent pace, with six charters concluded already. Locations, durations and assets vary a lot, but there's clearly momentum out there. For this quarter's focus, we dust off our crystal ball and ask: will the second half of 2026 be just as busy – and if so, **where, why, how and for whom**?

**Where?** We've recently **reviewed close to 280 projects**. Of these, around 130 are effectively dead, 70-80 are in recovery mode, leaving 70-80 projects with a genuine lease on life. We'd write off another 30 or so as unlikely to progress, which leaves **40-50 projects with a real shot at reaching FID**. Geographically, South/Southeast Asia, Europe and South America lead the pack – no great surprise, since Asia still has plenty of untapped potential and South America's gas consumption keeps climbing. Europe is a bit of a wildcard, but a handful of solid projects there should materialize in 2029-2030, meaning decisions need to land in 2026-2027.

**Why?** Economics usually take the wheel once a project reaches a certain stage, but the softer, qualitative factors matter just as much for commercial viability. Our research points to regulatory environment and existing infrastructure as the main sticking points – essentially, the need to build mooring/jetties and pipeline onshore, all under tight environmental rules. If you spend your days in FSRU business development, you'll know these as the usual "red flags" – ones that tend to get pushed aside once long-tenor contracts and strategic importance enter the conversation. Around **60% of our likely projects sit in challenging regulatory environments** (translation: plenty of room for external curveballs), yet **80% come with expected contract durations of 10+ years** (translation: a nice cushion against residual risk for the FSRU provider). Striking the right balance between these two forces is really what makes or breaks a project.

**How?** Newbuilds look set to stay in limited demand, reserved for a select 10-15% of projects – usually driven by project-specific quirks (Poland being a good example) or simply over-engineering (looking at you, SLNG). Conversions, thanks to their shorter lead times, lower costs and performance that holds its own against newbuilds, remain the go-to solution across our likely-list. A few projects could still go either way, but these TBC cases also rank low on our likelihood scale, so they don't move the needle much.

**Who?** Some projects in our database already have a likely FSRU provider lined up; others are still very much up for grabs. The "Big Boys" have already secured around 20-25% of our likely projects – a nod to some sharp, forward-leaning BD teams. That said, roughly two-thirds of the list is still open season. Cross-referencing project likelihood against probable provider, it's clear the Big Boys have generally locked in the opportunities most likely to succeed.

The final piece of the puzzle is **WHEN** all this actually comes to fruition and gets awarded – and for that conversation, we'd love to talk it through with you directly.

### FSRU activity and volumes

After three consecutive quarters of record volumes, **Q2/26 showed a drop in volumes** delivered to FSRUs. However, it is still the **highest Q2 on record** – which is interesting in the context of the supply disruptions witnessed across the wider market. A total of **17.2mt / 280 cargoes** (up 13% Y-o-Y from 15.3mt / 255 cargoes in Q2/25 and down 10% Q-o-Q from 19mt / 303 cargoes in Q1/26. Activity was higher in all regions apart from Middle East and Mediterranean (which means **Turkey took the foot seasonally off the throttle**). In fact, Turkey is – as highlighted before - becoming a substantial swing consumer with imports in Q2/26 at only 0.4mt compared to 3.4 in Q1/26 and 2.3 in Q4/25. European demand remains relatively steady hovering around 7mt during the quarter and has done so for the past year as all FSRUs (almost) implemented on back of Russia-Ukraine in 2022 are operational now. Americas saw a nudge higher as Argentina restarts seasonal imports with the **EXPEDIENT** locked in for another two-year period. However, it will be interesting to see how Argentina handles their LNG imports to serve Buenos Aires when the first FLNG arrives next year. Lastly, **Egypt is relentlessly importing** at a high pace with **3.1mt or some 50 cargoes** for the quarter and no anomalies on the FSRU operating side. A recent requirement for an FSU for 3-months to EGAS suggests maybe their ADPs are a bit more than what they are able to handle on the regas side.

There were no new additions to the FSRU fleet in terms of new operational terminals during the quarter, but we count 46 active terminals of which 44 received cargoes during the quarter; **ALEXANDROUPOLIS** and Abu Dhabi **EXPRESS** being the only “donut”-terminals in Q2. The busiest terminals were Eemshaven (Netherlands) **ENERGOS IGLOO** at 17 cargoes, followed by Cartagena (Colombia) **HOEGH GRACE** at 16 cargoes and Ain Sokhna Sumed (Egypt) **HOEGH GALLEON** at 15 cargoes. An interesting observation in Q2 was that Bangladesh kept the pace despite being FM’ed across the majority of their long-term SPAs with **Petrobangla FSRU** taking 13 cargoes and **Summit FSRU** taking 14 cargoes.

In terms of cargo sizes we note that Europe, Middle East and Mediterranean are taking full cargoes at appx 150,000-160,000cbm lots delivered whereas **Americas is very much a partial discharge destination** with the average size of 113,000cbm for the quarter. Asian cargoes were also this quarter substantially smaller at106,000cbm on average versus the 110,000cbm on average during the preceeding 12 months – however this is partially skewed lower by some smaller FSRUs in the region as the largest cargoes were just shy of 160,000cbm. We take the opportunity yet again to remind the industry that the **170,000+cbm storage requirement may in many cases be a technical perversion rather than an operational requirement**.

There are now nearly no “insurance FSRUs” in the market as most take a cargo every now and then, but Aqaba (Jordan) **ENERGOS FORCE**, Sergipe (Brazil) **ENERGOS NANOOK**, Dortyol (Turkey) **ETUGRUL GAZI**, Acu (Brazil) **BW MAGNA**, Sepetiba (Brazil) **KARMOL ASIA**, Hong Kong **BAUINIA SPIRIT** and Santos (Brazil), **HOEGH GIANT** each only taking one single cargo in the quarter. Hence, 7 units have crews with plenty of time to do maintenance. TGS remains “in service, but out of service” by the lack of an FSRU at the terminal – but this is expected to change with Ambar having announced a lease from NFE.

From a global perspective, **FSRUs delivered an all-time high 17.4% of all LNG volumes imported in Q2/26**. Global LNG imports dropped to 98.6mt in Q2 vs the current record 117.1mt in Q1. As such, the FSRUs were less impacted by the loss of Qatari LNG – which may not come as a surprise in light of the diverse geographical spread of FSRUs and their respective suppliers. We continue to see FSRUs growing in relevance and with the pipeline of projects and the associated expected demand profiles of the projects we’d be confident to expect FSRUs to provide 20% of global LNG imports within 2-3 years.

*Figure: FSRU Newbuild prices*

The steady stream of requirements is driving interest in additional tonnage and we currently count **four newbuilds and three firm conversions** in the orderbook. However, adding the units that are expected to firm up upon satisfaction of CPs we can **add another five conversions** – and then another **2-3 semi-speculative units**. Hence, the potential additional supply at present stands at 13-14 units. Putting that in the context of a fleet of just north of 50 units one may argue the early warnings go off for those thinking orderbook as % of fleet. **However, the different specifications, sizes and to a certain extent bespoke units renders the supply risk somewhat lower.**

Looking ahead we expect to see **at least one more newbuild and could easily count north of 10 projects targeting conversions.** Timing on this may not be 2026, but probably over the next 12 months to reach the targeted CODs. This marks an uptick on our numbers from last quarterly update. The **conversion cases** are interesting as they are mainly a **consequence of the availability of existing tonnage being too late** (and some project specific requirements), but we may ultimately find ourselves very long FSRUs in 2032-2034 in the absence of FSRU stickiness.

**Asset values have moved sideways for ST tonnage while DF tonnage is sliding lower** on back of a few concluded transactions. S&P activity during the quarter has mainly been for trading tonnage and not many LNGCs sold with conversion in mind (we count two sales that are likely conversion-linked) and we continue to be amazed how active KYC-challenged Buyers are. Maybe more surprising are those selling to entities that technically qualify, but are only halfway houses to a Russian flag.

**Preference on size is becoming very visible** and anything below 145,000 cbm is completely yesterday – as **project Buyers focus on 150,000+.** The challenge faced by several is whether to go 150,000-153,000 ST in the 30s or take the leap to 155, 160 or 165 DF where the value of larger storage and existing DF engines balance against the higher pricing which runs from US$ 70-80 million all the way up to US$ 140 million. Not to speak about those focusing on modern 170,000+ who may need to shell out additional green.

Delivery times are generally on forward basis with willingness to conclude pricing on the prompt; an element that FSRU/FSU projects offer to Sellers that we think Sellers have not fully appreciated just yet. The ability to secure your residual of an asset that by most hallmarks will be borderline obsolete in two years (when a mild 200+ additional 2-strokes have delivered and squeezed DFs into more niche trades should be welcomed with open arms – not a “we’re sellers US$ 5-10 million above last”. 

On the supplier side we see **engines as becoming the new critical path item**. Not necessarily due to the time it takes to produce, but the **availability of slots**. Regas modules are **still in the range of 14-18 months** (some quote less, but we find it hard to believe without having secured booster pumps speculatively). The consequence is that there are now **two items that are tricky to deal with on the conversion timeline**, but they are fortunately somewhat separate in the overall process. It also speaks in favor of considering DF tonnage instead of STs. **Total conversion time** from FID to redelivery from yard is as far as we read the EPC contractors still in the range of 23-28 months which means around **25-31 months FID to COD**. Capex also largely sits unchanged, but we’ve seen some early indications of a nudge up on costs. For the time being our range from last quarter remains; **US$ 110-140 million for DFs and US$ 140-180 for STs basis 500/750 SW/Glycol Open Loop** without any additional BOG handling, power supply, boilers or other project-specific bells and whistles.  

Lastly, the **FSRU newbuild market** seems to be getting a bit more traction with MISC ordering their Petronas-linked unit at Samsung at a quoted US$328 million and talks of Poland #2 as well as AKR heading down the newbuild route. This is give or take aligned with our most recent quote of US$ 335m for a “standard” newbuild (170-174,000cbm / 3 x 250MMSCFD SW-Glycol Open Loop). There are many benefits to the newbuilds vs conversions – and for those chasing 170,000cbm as an absolute requirement the price differential is marginal considering useful life. However, what still favors conversions is the lead time due to the fact that **newbuilds now can deliver in Q4/29 at the earliest.**
