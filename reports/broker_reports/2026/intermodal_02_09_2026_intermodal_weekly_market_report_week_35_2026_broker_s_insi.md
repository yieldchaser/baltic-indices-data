Market Insight  
By Nikos Tagoulis, Senior Analyst 
The EUA market maintained its upward trend in August, despite 
the quieter trading conditions and lower liquidity that typically 
characterize the holiday period. The advance extended the recov-
ery that began in late March and left the EU ETS on a constructive 
footing heading into autumn. The Front Dec -26 EUA contract av-
eraged €82.36 /ton during August and at the time of writing read 
€83.30 /ton, around 6.5% above the 2026 y -t-d average. Prices 
remained within a relatively narrow range during the month, and 
the contract continued to trade above its 50 -, 100- and 200-day 
moving averages.  
The European gas market has contributed to the firming of EUAs. 
Prices at the Dutch TTF hub rose significantly as geopolitical ten-
sions leading to Qatari volumes off market, low European storage 
levels and competition from Asian buyers for LNG cargoes height-
ened concerns over winter supply. 
Although the correlation between front-month TTF gas prices and 
EUAs fluctuated in August, it has strengthened recently, suggest-
ing that prevailing gas -market dynamics are supporting EUA de-
mand. The principal transmission channel is fuel -switching eco-
nomics: higher gas prices make coal more competitive with gas 
for power generation, encouraging more carbon -intensive output 
and increasing utilities’ demand for allowances. 
Furthermore, recent economic signals add to this supportive 
backdrop, pointing to firm industrial demand. The August euro-
zone manufacturing PMI reached its strongest level in more than 
four years, accompanied by stronger new orders, a recovery in 
exports and the first increase in factory employment after more 
than three years. Together, these developments strengthen the 
outlook for EUA demand. 
Despite the positive backdrop, traders remain cautious. Invest-
ment funds have reduced their net -long exposure in recent 
weeks, while commercial participants have continued to increase 
their forward exposure, reflecting hedging strategies built around 
expectations of further EUA price increases. The divergence indi-
cates that commercial buying remains driven by risk management 
even as speculative interest weakens. Compliance buying adds 
another source of demand ahead of September 30, including from 
shipping companies as the maritime sector ’s obligations continue 
to phase in. In 2026, shipping companies must surrender allow-
ances covering 70% of their verified 2025 emissions within the 
scope of the EU ETS, rising to 100% of 2026 emissions in the fol-
lowing year, with higher EUA prices increasing the compliance 
cost of voyages covered by the scheme.  
Looking ahead, policy is re -emerging as a major driver. The Com-
mission’s July proposal for the post -2030 EU ETS would slow the 
contraction of the emissions cap, extend conditional free alloca-
tion and ease the operation of the Market Stability Reserve. The 
measures would leave more allowances in circulation than under 
the present framework, easing concerns about future supply con-
straints. The initial market response was positive, however, this is 
only the starting point for further negotiations, leaving scope for 
revision. 
Beyond the EU ETS reform, the proposed linkage with the UK ETS 
remains an open issue. A summit expected to advance negotia-
tions in July was postponed following the change in UK leader-
ship, leaving the timetable uncertain. Progress towards linkage 
should narrow the spread between UKAs and EUAs as UK allow-
ance prices move closer to EU levels. Once implemented, the link-
age could become a bearish factor for EUA prices by expanding 
the pool of available allowances, although its impact would de-
pend on supply conditions across both systems. 
Concluding, the EUA outlook for the remainder of 2026 will de-
pend largely on developments across the European energy com-
plex. Geopolitical uncertainty, disruption to Middle Eastern LNG 
flows and Europe’s efforts to replenish low gas storage levels will 
shape gas and power prices, with changes in fuel -switching eco-
nomics feeding through to carbon. In the near term, compliance 
buying, improving industrial activity and commercial hedging 
should underpin EUA prices, while progress on EU ETS reform and 
EU–UK linkage will shape the longer-term direction.

Crude tanker spot markets eased overall this week, with Su-
ezmaxes suffering the largest losses while VLCCs posted modest 
gains. The BDTI averaged 2,855, down 3% w-o-w. 
Within the VLCC segment, regional performance was mixed. The 
Middle East market remained stable, supported by the growing 
use of AIS -dark transits through Hormuz, followed by STS trans-
fers off Fujairah or Sohar. Conditions stayed healthy, with steady 
enquiry and balanced tonnage availability. West Africa spot rates 
retreated w -o-w despite limited tonnage, as demand faded pro-
gressively through the week. The US Gulf opened firmly, carrying 
forward the previous week ’s momentum, but a series of failed 
fixtures pulled rates lower and weighed on sentiment. Meanwhile, 
owners sought longer -period employment for their tonnage. 
VLCC TCE rates averaged $334,066/day, up 3% w-o-w. 
The expected retreat following the elevated rates of previous 
weeks materialized for Suezmaxes, shifting negotiating power 
towards charterers. Rates corrected sharply in the Americas, 
Black Sea and West Africa as demand and fixing activity weak-
ened, leaving ample tonnage chasing limited cargoes. In the Black 
Sea, some owners avoided the region in search of safer and more 
economically attractive employment, while at USG, Suezmaxes 
lost cargoes to more competitive Aframaxes. MEG remained 
broadly stable. Average Suezmax TCE earnings fell 34% w -o-w to 
$184,914/day. 
The Aframax segment also moved lower, mainly due to subdued 
conditions at USG. In the Mediterranean, enquiry was brisk early 
in the week but gradually lost momentum, however TD19 
(CrossMed) proved resilient. With the US market still muted, own-
ers kept their vessels in the area, anticipating stronger activity 
from several Mediterranean ports next week. The North Sea fol-
lowed a similar pattern, opening firmly before activity slowed to-
wards the end of the week as Monday ’s UK bank holiday ap-
proached. Average Aframax TCE earnings stood at $68,644/day, 
down 15% on a weekly basis.

The dry bulk market strengthened last week, with broad -based 
gains across all segments, led by Capesizes. The BDI averaged 
3,031, up 7.5% w-o-w.  
Capesizes recorded the sharpest increase, with both the Atlantic 
and Pacific basins advancing. In the Pacific, steady miner cargoes 
underpinned activity. The Atlantic provided the stronger impetus, 
as fresh cargo volumes tightened the market and lifted rates, 
supported by enquiries from West Africa and South Brazil. De-
mand moderated towards the end of the week, but a limited pool 
of available units maintained upward pressure on rates, kept 
owners in a strong position and sustained firm market sentiment.  
The Panamax/Kamsarmax market also advanced, supported by a 
broad rise in cargo demand rather than any single regional driver. 
In the Pacific, North Pacific grain volumes increased as the new 
harvest entered export channels, while Australian mineral ship-
ments provided support and typhoon -related delays constrained 
vessel supply. Atlantic conditions were similarly positive, with 
firmer transatlantic cargoes and robust South American demand. 
Momentum may ease after recent gains, but limited tonnage 
should sustain owners’ leverage.  
The Supramax segment was broadly stable last week, with only 
minor rate movements amid subdued activity across several re-
gions. In the Pacific, tonnage was absorbed by increased NoPac 
grain enquiries, stabilizing rates. Atlantic sentiment improved as 
stronger US Gulf activity attracted units, although North America 
remained quiet. In Mediterranean, conditions weakened affected 
by scarce Black Sea grain shipments, while in Asia, Indonesian 
cargoes to the Subcontinent thinned prompt tonnage list.  
The Handysize market posted modest gains, led by firm funda-
mentals in the US Gulf, amid robust demand and limited vessel 
availability. East Coast South America provided another area of 
strength amid increased enquiries. In Asia, healthy demand grad-
ually reduced the tonnage pool, lending further support. South 
Atlantic witnessed a gradual weakening of activity.

Ship recycling markets remain broadly healthy, but a shortage 
of available tonnage continues to constrain activity. Recyclers 
retain a strong appetite, yet robust freight rates across several 
vessel classes are leading owners to defer scrapping decisions 
and take advantage of the favorable market conditions. The 
supply outlook should improve as vessels age and freight mar-
kets normalize, prompting a larger flow of recycling candidates.  
At Alang, the market remained stable, with recyclers actively 
seeking available candidates and focusing on niche units. This 
focus continues to sustain activity amid a shortage of main-
stream tonnage, while India remains less competitive than its 
subcontinent rivals but is trying to build on its compliance ad-
vantage, since most of the sector is HKC -certified. Firmer do-
mestic steel prices and a partial recovery in the rupee are also 
improving market fundamentals and sentiment. 
Chattogram had a slow week, held back by the shortage of fresh 
candidates and weak conditions in the local steel market, where 
tepid demand for finished products has left mills reluctant buy-
ers. Sentiment was further weighed down by a weakening of 
local currency, with inflationary pressure from elevated energy 
costs adding to the strain. Buying appetite remains in place, 
though it seems that a meaningful influx of fresh tonnage is 
needed before activity can lift. 
Pakistan is holding a steady pace, with a degree of activity and 
candidate circulation still evident. The local steel market is flat, 
heavy monsoon rains and a broader economic slowdown having 
curbed demand. On the policy front, recyclers have secured 
agreement from the authorities that domestically sourced scrap 
will be recognized within the electricity -based sales tax mecha-
nism. Until now only imported scrap allowed them to reclaim 
tax. Local material therefore bore a hidden cost, which weighed 
on the prices yards could offer. 
Aliaga remains muted and little changed in the week, with the 
Lira continuing to weaken. More positively, the local steel mar-
ket has shown some improvement, with mills firming their offer 
prices for scrap.
