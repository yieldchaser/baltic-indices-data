MARKET OVERVIEW
A sharp re-escalation in Middle Eastern geopolitical volatility as a fresh wave of military strikes has
reignited direct conflict along the Persian Gulf, reversing weeks of relative stability. Following locali sed
strikes against coastal installations, security forces targeted two commercial vessels under a newly
implemented reciprocal deterrence policy after outbound crude carriers were struck by airborne
projectiles. In immediate retaliation, missile and drone salvos were launched toward regional staging hubs
hosting international forces across neighbouring territories. This rapid return to open hostilities has
abruptly curtailed hopes for a diplomatic breakthrough, driving global crude futures to multi -week highs
with international benchmarks crossing US$95 a barrel. With worldwide commercial stockpiles and
strategic reserves heavily depleted after months of ongoing disruption, energy markets are factoring in a
renewed structural risk premium, even as downstream domestic refiners run at extraordinary utilisation
rates to capture soaring fuel margins.
Despite the heightened security risks, significant energy volumes continue to move through the corridor
via tightly managed operations. Official monitoring reports indicate that seaborne outflows from the Strait
of Hormuz are averaging roughly 8 million barrels per day, bolstered by an estimated 5 million daily
barrels moving through regional pipeline bypasses. While international naval escorts have shepherded
dozens of laden tankers across the chokepoint to maintain supply continuity to primary Asian consumers,
local defence officials have asserted that the waterway remains under strict surveillance. F leet operators
continue to exercise extreme caution, noting that while physical volumes are escaping through
specialised shuttle arrangements and transponder manipulations, the threat of drone attacks, drifting sea
mines, and localised interdictions keeps maritime insurance rates and spot tanker fixtures elevated.
Broader shifts in energy diplomacy are redrawing trade routes across the Western Hemisphere. A
deepened bilateral agreement granting substantial operational stakes and preferential crude purchasing
rights in Venezuelan oil fields to North American interests is reshaping Latin American flows. This
reorientation directly impacts long-standing transpacific trade patterns, where East Asian buyers
previously absorbed most local heavy crude exports to settle historical loan facilities. While existing
commercial entanglements ensure that major Asian entities remain embedded across South American
infrastructure, the loss of discounted heavy crude access has forced independent regional refiners to seek
costlier alternatives from Atlantic and northern suppliers. As Western security policies increasingly
emphasise regional economic alignment, participants are anticipating long-term structural changes in
tanker routing and commodity credit settlements across the Pacific basin .

DRY BULK | BDI: 3,628 ▲13.87% (w-o-w)
Dry bulk sector demonstrated notable strength heading into early September, advancing on broad- based
cargo demand and localised fleet bottlenecks even as wider geopolitical disruptions continue to
challenge international trade corridors. Lingering instability in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov has severely
restricted regional agricultural outflows—with monthly wheat shipments dropping to under 2.5 million tons
compared to 6.3 million tons during the same period last year—while the ongoing closure of the Strait of
Hormuz has stalled roughly a quarter of global fertiliser trade and left around 120 to 150 bulk carriers
immobilised.
Despite these headwinds, BDI surged 13.87% end week to reach 3,628 points, and stands as the highest
level seen since December 2023. This upward momentum was spearheaded by the Capesize segment,
where the Capesize index jumped 20.4% to 6,427 points—as aggressive chartering from the three major
mining houses in Australia-to-China routes alongside tight vessel supply in the Atlantic basin. Medium
and smaller bulkers participated fully in the rally; the Panamax index extended its positive run into an
eighth consecutive session by climbing to 2,448 points, supported by a domestic accident in China that
prompted immediate interest in seaborne coking coal, while the Supramax index edged up to 1,67 5 points.
Segment Highlights

T/A earnings to US$55,050’s and Pacific R/V to US$52,150’s per day. However, market sentiment remains
cautious heading into late September laycans, as an influx of ballasters toward Brazil and the clearing of
Chinese port bottlenecks threaten to ease near-term supply tightness against limited fresh demand.
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax firmed steadily across both basins this week, supported by early vessel
bookings for upcoming grain bookings despite current export dips, while a drop in Pacific ballasters and
tightening prompt tonnage left charterers chasing owners' ideas. Although sluggish coal demand and low
river water levels in Kalimantan restricted Indonesian coal flows, replenished Atlantic cargo book helped
underpin positive forward sentiment. Spot returns pushed higher across key Atlantic routes, lifting Brazil
R/V to US$22,100’s and F/H trips to US$30,950’s per day.
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax market edged up this week in a mixed and largely directionless session,
as firm enquiry for NOPAC grain and USG export stems countered softer regional pockets. However,
broader gains were restrained by an influx of ballast tonnage across the Atlantic alongside a decline in
Indonesian coal exports caused by low river levels hindering barge operations. Spot earnings adjusted
across key trades, lifting T/A rates to US$28,350’s and Pacific R/V to US$17,150’s per day.
Handysize: Handysize traded in a generally subdued fashion this week, though healthy underlying
fundamentals in parts of the Pacific and South Atlantic continued to lend baseline support. The USG  held
relatively firm conditions alongside an active appetite for period business, which helped counterbalance
persistent softness across the Mediterranean. Spot earnings reflected these mixed market dynamics, with
T/A rates advancing to US$16,950’s while Inter-Pacific runs eased slightly to US$16,400’s per day.

Market Insights
Alang, India: Alang maintained a resilient outlook, buoyed by a noticeable strengthening in local trading
conditions. Firm domestic steel values and a recovering rupee have combined to lift morale, prompting
local recyclers to improve their commercial bids. However, the broader subcontinent market faces an
ongoing shortage of conventional recycling candidates, as strong global freight earnings continu e to
incentivise shipowners to keep older vessels trading rather than committing them to the beaches. While
rising domestic appetite has nudged Alang buyers toward better levels, a regional spread persists, leaving
most dry bulk and tanker tonnage largely out of reach as open-market candidates gravitate toward
higher price points in Chattogram and Gadani. Instead, Indian plot owners are directing their purchasing
energy towards niche units.
Official figures released this week revealed that India’s GDP expanded by a robust 7.8% in the first fiscal
quarter, outpacing central bank estimates on the back of double-digit investment growth, heavy
government infrastructure outlays, and resilient services. While manufacturing purchasing activity saw a
mild seasonal moderation in August, headline industrial confidence remains steady as input cost inflation
recedes to multi-month lows.

Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram settled into a measured rhythm, with overall outlook showing
minimal change from preceding weeks. Operational tempo across the beaches remained somewhat
subdued as buyers focused on cutting previously delivered inventory, while the complete absence of fresh
market sales left yards without immediate price direction. While fundamental buying appetite is still
present, sentiment lacks forceful conviction amid quiet downstream steel demand, ongoing currency
softness, and high industrial power costs that continue to squeeze operational margins.
Furthermore, with the market’s more aggressive cash buyers having largely fulfilled their immediate
tonnage commitments, secondary yard operators are stepping in with more conservative bids, preventing
any sudden upward momentum in headline vessel price indications.
The latest figures from Bangladesh Bank confirm that external liquidity remains resilient, with gross foreign
exchange reserves holding firmly around US$36.4 billion, comfortably above US$31.6 billion under the IMF’s
BPM6 accounting standard, bolstered by consistent remittance flows and a steady balance of payments
surplus. Headline consumer price inflation showed welcome signs of cooling, easing to an eight -month
low of 8.32% in July as food and logistics costs moderated.

Gaddani, Pakistan: Gadani maintained a relatively firm position, although vessel purchase indications
witnessed a mild correction from their recent highs. The earlier surge in prices appears to have been
largely a knee-jerk reaction, driven by a small group of recyclers facing individual buying pressures and a
strong need to secure tonnage. With these requirements now largely covered, pricing has returned to
more realistic and sustainable levels in line with prevailing domestic fundamentals.
Looking ahead, the key concern will be, at the time of delivery, the handling of vessels acquired at elevated
prices that are not fully supported by the underlying steel market. Such purchases could create some
“song and dance” at the waterfront, with even minor vessel related issues potentially being used as a
pretext to reopen negotiations or seek downward price adjustments at the time of delivery.
But generally, the sentiment has received a substantial structural boost from tax authorities recognising
domestically generated scrap within the electricity consumption-based sales tax framework, resolving a
major compliance hurdle and encouraging local recyclers to fulfil their procurement targets with renewed
confidence.
Data released this week by the State Bank of Pakistan revealed that the central bank’s foreign exchange
reserves rose to US$17.12 billion by late August, pushing the country’s total liquid foreign currency reserves
past US$22.53 billion. Although headline inflation ticked up to 11.1% in August due to seasonal food and
energy pressures, recent sovereign credit rating upgrades by major international agencies and an
anchored benchmark policy rate of 11.5% continue to reinforce banking confidence. With foreign exchange
reserves providing nearly three months of import cover, local commercial banks are maintaining a steady
flow of LCs.

SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS
India: The imported containerised scrap market firmed day-on-day, highlighted by a 10-FCL HMS booking
at US$350/t CFR Mundra. Workable HMS levels for 23/24 MT loads ranged between US $355-365/t CFR for
arrival-based terms, with documents-against-payment options securing a US$10-12/t discount.
Meanwhile, UK-origin offers held firm, circulating at US$412-415/t CFR for shredded scrap and US$360-
365/t CFR for HMS.
Pakistan: Imported shredded trading remained subdued despite a confirmed deal for UK -origin material
booked at US$415/t CFR Qasim. General offers held stubbornly above US$420/t CFR as ongoing container
shortages, surging freight rates, and a stronger British pound kept landed import costs elevated. These
logistical and currency pressures continued to restrain broad buying appetite across regional mills.
Bangladesh: Import values held firm, underpinned by robust domestic scrap prices hovering at BDT
54,000-55,000/t. US bulk offers remained steady near US$390/t CFR, though escalating global freight rates
prevented major mills from translating active inquiries into firm bookings. Meanwhile, Japanese H2 offers
stood above US$385/t CFR, though softer domestic pricing in Japan is expected to exert downward
pressure shortly.
Turkiye: Deep-sea import benchmarks climbed day-on-day on 2 September, driven by higher rebar
valuations and healthier mill margins. Bullish sellers targeted US$386/t CFR for US material and US$380/t
CFR for EU cargoes, with Baltic-origin expectations rising to US$381-382/t CFR. Strong market sentiment
suggests US-origin HMS 80:20 could challenge US$385-386/t CFR shortly and potentially approach
US$390/t CFR next week.

Commodities Focus
In the ferrous sector, iron ore futures advanced toward CNY 730 per ton to secure a second straight weekly
increase, bolstered by climbing ocean shipping expenses and a fourth consecutive weekly drawdown in
Chinese portside stocks, even as Brazilian producer Usiminas halted operations at its Samambaia facility
on September 2 due to unviable freight and price dynamics. This feedstock strength has created
significant friction for Chinese steelmakers, keeping domestic rebar futures pinned near CNY 3,100 per ton
as average mill deficits in Tangshan broadened past CNY 100 per ton and the non-manufacturing PMI
stalled at 49.0—its softest reading since December 2022—though outbound steel shipments provided a
bright spot by expanding 7.8% week-on-week to 2.55 million tons by late August.
Concurrently, copper futures consolidated near US$6.57 per pound on Friday as extensive physical
bottlenecks continued to tighten global availability, highlighted by an export embargo from the
Democratic Republic of Congo, a 9.4% year-on-year contraction in Chile’s July output from harsh weather
and repairs, and a 3% dip in China's June refined metal volume to 1.1 million tons. Meanwhile, the global
energy complex saw thermal coal advance to roughly US$145 per ton, touching an eleven-week peak as
persistent Middle Eastern tensions and constrained liquefied natural gas shipments reinforced fuel -
switching needs; and although solar installations in China recently overtook coal capacity for the first time,
coal remains the cornerstone of global baseload power, generating nearly 11,000 TWh in 2026 and
expected by the International Energy Agency to preserve its leading power generation role through 2030.
