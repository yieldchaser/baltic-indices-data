## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# FINDINGS LAND, MARKET COOLS

The investigation into August’s fatal recycling incident has moved from open questions to formal ***Findings land.*** findings. The Ministry of Industries inquiry identified shortcomings in safety supervision and gas- testing procedures, including hydrogen sulphide risk arising from stagnant ballast water that was not adequately captured by the existing gas-free regime. The report recommended action under the prevailing rules and stronger recycling-certification requirements for vessels from non-HKC flag states, while the Industries Minister said responsibility and any penalties would proceed through the appropriate legal process. The industry implication is wider than one facility. Procedures around ballast spaces, atmosphere testing and high-risk work are now firmly under review.

Commercially, Chattogram is showing precisely why port activity and fresh sales must be kept ***Pipeline clears.*** separate. The 27,824 LDT FPSO Glow, shown as an arrival last week, was delivered on August 30, while the 3,846 LDT bulker Orange Link was delivered on August 29. The waterfront is therefore consuming previously secured tonnage, but there were no fresh market sales this week. The hotter buyers have covered part of their immediate requirements, leaving the next tier to quote at lower levels.

Local steel offered no argument against that correction, but no support for another rally either. ***Urgency fades.*** Plate remained unchanged throughout the week at BDT 64,000 per ton, approximately USD 522.24 per ton. The Taka moved favourably, with Bangladesh Bank’s latest available spot reference strengthening to around BDT 122.79 to the Dollar from the 123.07 area reported last week. Currency therefore improved marginally while the vessel market softened, confirming that this week’s move is about fulfilled demand rather than deteriorating local fundamentals. Chattogram remains operational, capable and interested in tonnage. What has changed is urgency. The post-monsoon beach is working, recently secured ships are moving and local steel is stable, but the strongest buyers no longer need to chase every available candidate. Fresh supply remains thin enough to prevent any collapse. The beach is working. The bid is waiting.

# <u>INDIA</u>

# BACKING BUILDS, ALANG LIFTS

Alang received its most important structural endorsement in some time this week. BIMCO, European Shipowners (ECSA), ICS, INTERCARGO, INTERTANKO and the World Shipping Council jointly ***Backing widens.*** welcomed the European Commission’s proposal to include two Indian recycling facilities on the European List and urged policymakers to approve the updated List swiftly. The proposal is not yet the same thing as final listing, but the breadth of industry support matters. India’s compliance case is no longer being advanced from the waterfront alone. Commercially, buyers have also improved their posture. Growing demand has pushed Alang recyclers toward better levels, although standard dry and tanker pricing remains behind both Pakistan and Bangladesh. The latest port report now records the 18,848 LDT LPG carrier Ble In as delivered on August 26, advancing the specialist-tonnage story that carried through August. Gas carriers, non-ferrous-rich units, green ships and other higher-value candidates continue to give India a market of its own even where straightforward sub-continent competition remains difficult.

Local fundamentals strengthened again. Alang plate opened at INR 42,000 per ton, moved to INR 42,100 and held there through Friday. More importantly, the strengthening Rupee lifted the USD ***Plate firms.*** equivalent from approximately USD 440.25 to USD 445.69 per ton over the week. USD/INR moved to around 94.49 by Friday, giving recyclers a meaningful improvement in Dollar purchasing power. The published GMS desk board may not have moved this week, but the economics underneath the bid certainly have.

India remains third for conventional tonnage, and that distinction should not be blurred. Yet while its two competitors are normalising after satisfying immediate demand, Alang is moving in the opposite ***Demand improves.*** direction. Better buying appetite, firmer steel, a stronger Rupee and growing support for the EU List proposal do not erase the competitive gap, but they are narrowing the handicap. The conventional board still says third. The direction says watch it.

# <u>PAKISTAN</u>

# DEMAND FILLED, PRICES RESET

Two weeks ago the bid outran the hulls. Last week the hulls arrived. This week they are beginning ***Demand covered.*** to satisfy the requirement. Gadani remains the top-priced sub-continent destination, but levels have cooled significantly over the past fortnight as the most aggressive recyclers covered their immediate needs. The correction should not be mistaken for disappearing demand. It is the normalisation that follows a buying run in which too many yards were competing for too few ships.

The waterfront increasingly explains the change. The 8,128 LDT container vessel Visakha, ***Hulls feed.*** previously recorded as an arrival, was delivered on August 30. The 5,688 LDT bulker Lina F arrived on September 1, while the 2,632 LDT general cargo vessel Larus arrived on August 27. The 4,990 LDT tanker Lyra remains recorded as having arrived on August 21. Gadani is therefore no longer trying to satisfy pent-up demand with an empty beach. Actual tonnage is now feeding the system. Local fundamentals did not deteriorate alongside vessel pricing. Plate held at PKR 200,000 per ton, approximately USD 719.94 per ton, throughout the entire week, while the State Bank’s September 3 revaluation rate remained close to PKR 277.42 to the Dollar. Pakistan’s August CPI did, however, rebound sharply to 11.1% from 9.2% in July, returning some inflationary pressure to the domestic picture. The key commercial point remains straightforward: steel and currency have held. Vessel values have corrected because urgency has been satisfied.

Pakistan therefore stays at the top of the board, but the bidding hierarchy underneath the hottest ***Top spot holds.*** yards has changed. First-tier requirement has been partly filled; next-tier recyclers are considerably less inclined to reproduce August’s extremes. Candidate scarcity should keep a floor under the market, particularly for attractive units, but the next ship will have to find the buyer whose requirement has not already been met. The appetite remains. The urgency has changed hands.
